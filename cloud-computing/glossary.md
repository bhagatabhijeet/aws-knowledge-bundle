# Cloud Computing Glossary

| Term | Definition | Moving Day analogy |
|---|---|---|
| **On-premises** | Infrastructure a company owns and physically houses itself | A house you built and maintain yourself |
| **Traditional hosting** | Renting a fixed physical or dedicated server from a hosting company | A fixed house rented from a small landlord |
| **The Cloud** | On-demand computing resources rented from a provider over the internet, billed for what you use | An endlessly-expandable apartment complex |
| **Migration** | Moving a workload from on-premises/traditional hosting into the cloud | Moving day |
| **Lift-and-shift** | Migrating a workload with minimal changes, then improving it afterward | Moving your furniture in as-is, then redecorating room by room |
| **Elasticity / Scalability** | The ability to add or remove capacity as demand changes | The complex resizing your unit on demand |
| **IaaS (Infrastructure as a Service)** | Renting raw virtualized compute, storage, and networking; you manage the OS upward | A bare apartment unit — you bring your own furniture |
| **PaaS (Platform as a Service)** | A managed runtime environment; you deploy code, the provider manages the OS and runtime | A furnished, serviced apartment unit |
| **Serverless** | A model where you supply only the code, and the provider manages all infrastructure and scaling automatically | Booking a shared room by the hour, holding no unit at all |
| **Vendor lock-in** | Being dependent on one provider's specific tools/APIs in a way that makes switching costly | Only knowing how to work one specific complex's front desk |
| **Market leader (cloud context)** | The cloud provider with the largest share of the market, often setting the template others follow | The complex whose floor plan every other complex copied |
